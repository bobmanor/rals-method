# The RALS Method™

RALS is a four-movement communication method created by Bob Manor for turning a genuine observation into clear, grounded expression without taking the reader's agency away.

**Riff. Anchor. Lift. Shred.**

Between Anchor and Lift, a Handoff may occur: the reader recognizes themselves in what has been said and begins carrying the thought. Handoff is not a scripted fifth step. It is an emergent result of a truthful Riff and a credible Anchor.

RALS can help with essays, posts, talks, teaching, leadership communication, and AI-assisted writing. It is not a claim that every message must use the same shape, and it is not a substitute for evidence, judgment, or editing.

## The method in one minute

### Riff

Name the live observation, tension, question, or pattern. Start with what you genuinely noticed, not a manufactured hook.

### Anchor

Ground the observation in real firsthand experience, a verifiable example, or clearly identified evidence. Anchor answers, “Why are you in a position to notice this?” It must never invent credibility.

### Handoff

Leave enough truth and space for the recipient to connect the pattern to their own world. Do not write a “Handoff section.” If recognition happens, ownership starts to move from speaker to recipient.

### Lift

Develop the implication. Show what the observation may mean, what changes when it is seen clearly, or where the pattern may lead. Offer perspective rather than issuing a command.

### Shred

Give the reader something practical to take away. What can they do, try, question, change, or apply using what they've just learned? The takeaway should follow naturally from the meaning, without forcing a sale or demanding agreement.

## A simple first pass

Write four rough answers:

- What am I noticing?
- What have I personally seen, done, or verified that grounds it?
- What might another person recognize or conclude for themselves?
- What can someone actually do with this understanding?

Then revise the answers into one natural piece. The labels are for thinking; they do not need to appear in the finished writing.

## What RALS protects

- Truth before performance
- Firsthand grounding before borrowed authority
- Recipient agency before forced agreement
- Clarity before volume
- Voice and values before generic fluency
- Restraint before a compulsory CTA

## RALS alongside PAS and AIDA

PAS and AIDA remain useful when a writer needs a clear persuasive or transactional sequence. RALS serves a different primary purpose: helping lived observation transfer without manufacturing agitation, desire, or urgency. The methods can be complementary. A practical message may use RALS to establish a truthful point of view and a light AIDA or PAS sequence to explain a legitimate next step. See [EXAMPLES.md](EXAMPLES.md).

## AI use

AI can help extract source material, organize patterns, test clarity, and compress a draft. It must not manufacture the Anchor, impersonate lived experience, exaggerate certainty, or overwrite the author's voice. Polaris may provide richer voice, values, and identity context, but RALS does not depend on Polaris or any other proprietary process.
