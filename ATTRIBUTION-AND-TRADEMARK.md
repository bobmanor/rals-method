# Attribution and Trademark Guidance

This page provides practical publishing guidance, not legal advice. A qualified lawyer should review trademark and licensing questions that matter commercially.

## Attribution

For a short reference:

> RALS Method™ — Riff, Anchor, Lift, Shred — created by Bob Manor.

For a page, workshop, or adaptation:

> Based on the RALS Method™ — Riff, Anchor, Lift, Shred — created by Bob Manor. Source: [official repository URL]. Adapted by [name]; Bob Manor has not endorsed this adaptation unless expressly stated.

Good attribution should identify the method, creator, source, and material changes. It should not imply partnership, certification, endorsement, or approval.

## Trademark use

The ™ symbol communicates a claim to the name; it does not state that a mark is registered. Use ® only if the relevant mark is registered in the applicable jurisdiction and legal guidance supports that use.

Reasonable referential use includes:

- “an article written using the RALS Method™”
- “a workshop about the RALS Method™”
- “compatible with the RALS Method™”

Avoid naming an unrelated product or service in a way that suggests it is official, certified, or produced by Bob Manor. Do not use Bob Manor's name, logos, or other brand assets as your own branding.

## Suggested repository notice

> RALS Method™ is a trademark claimed by Bob Manor. The license for repository content, once selected, does not grant trademark rights or permission to imply endorsement. Nominative use for accurate reference and attribution is permitted to the extent allowed by law.

## Method, expression, and brand

Copyright, trademark, and other rights protect different things. A license for the text in this repository does not necessarily grant exclusive rights in the underlying idea or method, and it does not grant rights to use a trademark as a brand. This is one reason the content license and trademark notice should remain separate.

## Before publication

- Confirm how ownership should be identified: Bob Manor personally or a named company.
- Confirm the exact marks being claimed.
- Confirm whether any logos or design assets will be included and under what terms.
- Have counsel review the notice if certification, training, commercial licensing, or enforcement is planned.
