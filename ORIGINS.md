# Origins and Public Record

## Purpose of this record

This page documents the public articles that preceded this repository and the role each source plays in the development record. It establishes publication history and authorship context. It does not, by itself, prove originality, legal ownership, scientific validity, or effectiveness.

## Creator account

Bob Manor describes RALS as a method he noticed through repeated communication failures and adjustments rather than a formula he set out to invent. In his published origin account, he connects the method to feedback that his explanations were too verbose, his observation of listeners becoming overloaded, and a musical analogy: a solo gains force because of what comes before it.

That account names four movements—Riff, Anchor, Lift, and Shred—and places an invisible Handoff between Anchor and Lift. The Handoff is where ownership of the thought may begin to transfer to the recipient. The public account describes RALS as a communication framework grounded in lived experience, not merely a copywriting formula.

A public article in the RALS publication sequence. Preserve the page and its original publication context as part of the development record.

### 3. Beyond PAS and AIDA

https://www.influence.vin/blog/beyond-pas-and-aida

Published December 12, 2025. The article contrasts transaction-oriented copy with identity- and experience-grounded communication in an AI-mediated environment. This repository narrows one point: PAS and AIDA can remain useful and complementary when a legitimate persuasive or action-oriented task calls for them.

### 4. What the RALS Method™ Actually Delivers

https://www.influence.vin/blog/what-the-rals-method-delivers

Published January 30, 2026. The article emphasizes observation, lived experience, recipient participation, implication rather than instruction, restraint, and freedom from a compulsory CTA. It describes the Handoff as the fulcrum where the reader begins participating in the thought.

### 5. How the RALS Method Was Born

https://www.bobmanor.com/blog/how-rals-was-born

Published February 9, 2026. Bob Manor’s first-person origin account describes the feedback, attention patterns, musical analogy, and movement from proving intelligence toward transferring meaning. It explicitly names the invisible Handoff between Anchor and Lift.

## How this repository relates to the articles

The articles preserve the method's public evolution. This repository consolidates the current working specification for learning and application. Where phrasing or emphasis differs, the repository should not be used to silently rewrite the historical record. Significant changes to the specification should be dated and explained in version history.

## Citation suggestion

Until the repository has a permanent URL and release date, cite the relevant public article directly. After publication, a general citation can use:

> Manor, Bob. *The RALS Method™: Riff, Anchor, Lift, Shred.* GitHub repository, version [release], [year]. [repository URL].

Replace every bracketed field with verified publication information.
