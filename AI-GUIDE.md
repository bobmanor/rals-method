# Using the RALS Method with AI

## What AI should and should not do

Use ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini, or another capable assistant as an interviewer, organizer, pattern finder, drafting partner, and editor. Do not use it as a source of invented experience.

The assistant may:

- Ask for concrete memories and examples
- Extract candidate Riffs from supplied material
- Separate firsthand evidence from interpretation
- Group recurring themes across transcripts
- Suggest structures and alternate phrasings
- Identify generic, inflated, or repetitive language
- Run a Shred pass while preserving voice

The assistant must not:

- Invent an Anchor, quote, client, event, statistic, result, or credential
- Claim to know the author's feelings or intent when it does not
- convert uncertainty into certainty
- force a Handoff question or a CTA
- mimic a living writer or overwrite the user's natural voice
- treat Polaris as required

## Before drafting

Give the assistant only material you have the right to use. Remove confidential, personal, or identifying information when necessary. For consequential claims, keep a source list and verify the final wording yourself.

## Minimal context packet

You can begin without a brand system. Supply what you know:

```text
AUTHOR
Who I am and the relevant experience I actually have:

AUDIENCE
Who this is for and what relationship I have with them:

VOICE
Three to five qualities of my natural voice:
Words or habits I avoid:
One or two samples that sound like me:

VALUES AND BOUNDARIES
What I will not exaggerate, conceal, or pressure people to do:

SOURCE MATERIAL
Notes, transcript, draft, links, or the idea I want to explore:

PURPOSE AND FORMAT
What this piece needs to do, where it will appear, and any length limit:
```

If you have a Polaris profile, add the relevant voice, values, worldview, and boundary notes. Do not assume the assistant has access to Polaris, and do not include private material you are not comfortable sharing.

## Universal RALS prompt

```text
Help me develop this material using the RALS Method: Riff, Anchor, Lift, Shred.

Handoff is not a fifth step or a section to write. It is an emergent moment between Anchor and Lift when the recipient begins connecting the grounded observation to their own experience. Create room for it, but do not script it or tell the reader what to think.

Rules:
1. Preserve my voice, values, intended audience, and level of certainty.
2. Use only the facts and firsthand experiences I provide. Never invent an anecdote, quote, result, statistic, credential, source, or emotional reaction.
3. Distinguish facts, firsthand observations, interpretations, and forecasts.
4. If the Anchor is missing or weak, stop and ask focused questions before drafting.
5. Do not manufacture agitation, urgency, hype, or a CTA.
6. A CTA is allowed only if the stated purpose genuinely requires one; keep it clear and proportionate.
7. After drafting, run a Shred pass for repetition, generic AI language, inflated claims, and unnecessary explanation. Do not erase useful nuance or my natural irregularities.

First, return:
- candidate Riff
- available Anchor evidence
- missing facts or questions
- possible Lift
- voice risks

Wait for my answers if important grounding is missing. Then draft the piece without showing framework labels unless I ask for them.

[Paste context packet and source material]
```

## Workflow 1: Starting with one idea

Use the universal prompt, then add:

```text
My rough observation is: [idea].

Interview me before drafting. Ask one question at a time and prioritize:
- the moment I first noticed this
- a specific example
- what changed in my understanding
- where my experience ends
- what I want the recipient to be free to decide
```

Answer plainly. “I don't know” is useful; it prevents the assistant from filling gaps.

## Workflow 2: Starting with an interview or transcript

```text
Analyze the transcript below as source material, not as polished copy.

Create an evidence table with:
- exact source passage or timestamp
- candidate Riff
- firsthand Anchor
- interpretation or Lift
- uncertainty or verification needed
- distinctive voice language worth preserving

Do not draft yet. Flag every claim that appears to need confirmation and every anecdote that could identify another person. Then propose up to three truthful angles.

[Paste transcript]
```

Choose an angle and resolve the flagged items before asking for a draft.

## Workflow 3: Starting with many transcripts or documents

Work in stages. Do not ask the model to turn a large archive directly into finished thought leadership.

```text
Build a source map from these materials. For each recurring pattern, list:
- source file and location
- what was actually said or observed
- whether it is firsthand, secondhand, or external evidence
- contradictions or changes over time
- candidate Riff
- possible Anchor
- what cannot be claimed safely

Do not merge separate events into one story. Do not infer frequency from repetition in duplicated files. Return a shortlist of patterns with the strongest firsthand grounding.
```

Keep the source map. It is the audit trail for later drafting.

## Workflow 4: Revising an existing draft

```text
Review this draft using RALS, but preserve its purpose and my voice.

Mark, without rewriting first:
- the current Riff
- every Anchor and whether it is sufficiently specific
- where Handoff has room to emerge or is being over-scripted
- the Lift and whether it follows from the evidence
- Shred candidates
- unsupported or fabricated-sounding claims
- pressure, hype, or forced CTA language

Then propose the smallest set of changes. After I approve the direction, produce a clean revision.

[Paste draft]
```

## Workflow 5: Voice calibration without Polaris

Provide two to five samples you wrote without AI, ideally in the same format. Then ask:

```text
Build a provisional voice brief from these samples. Separate what you can directly observe from what you are inferring. Include rhythm, vocabulary, level of directness, use of examples, humor, endings, and phrases to avoid. Do not imitate quirks mechanically. Ask me to correct the brief before using it.
```

Revise the brief until it feels accurate. Voice calibration improves fidelity but does not create permission to invent facts.

## Workflow 6: Optional Polaris context

If a Polaris profile exists, extract only what is relevant to this piece:

- Core values and non-negotiables
- Recurring worldview or tensions
- Natural voice and vocabulary
- Audience relationship
- Relevant experience
- Boundaries, sensitivities, and claims to avoid

Tell the assistant that Polaris is context, not copy. The finished work must still be grounded in the current source material. RALS remains fully usable without Polaris.

## Fact and fidelity check

Before publishing, ask:

```text
Audit the draft. Produce four lists:
1. claims directly supported by my supplied material
2. interpretations that should remain clearly framed as interpretations
3. claims requiring external verification or a citation
4. language that may not sound like me

Do not quietly fix or invent support. Quote the relevant sentence from my draft and identify the source you relied on. If no source exists, say so.
```

Then verify the final version yourself. AI output is a proposal, not provenance.

## Shred prompt

```text
Run a conservative Shred pass. Remove repetition, throat-clearing, generic transitions, inflated certainty, performance, and pressure. Preserve concrete detail, qualifications, rhythm, and necessary context. Do not add a summary or CTA. Show a short change log after the clean revision.
```

## Troubleshooting

### The draft sounds generic

Return to the source material. Add a specific moment, decision, sensory detail, or phrase the author actually used. Do not solve generic writing with stronger adjectives.

### The Anchor sounds like a bio

Replace status claims with the relevant event or work. Explain what the author directly encountered and what it taught them.

### The model keeps adding a CTA

State the intended ending explicitly: “End when the implication is clear. Do not invite comments, subscriptions, calls, downloads, or purchases.”

### The Handoff feels scripted

Remove the rhetorical question or sentence that tells readers what they recognize. Let the grounded example and implication sit closer together with less explanation.

### The piece is too short after Shred

Do not restore filler. Add only missing grounding, useful distinction, or context the recipient needs.
